# Interaction in the Compose UI

This document describes how the `Terminal` composable of `vtfx-compose` handles the user's interaction: touch, the mouse, the selection and copying, links, hardware keys and their interception by the app, the extra key bar, cursor blinking, its own size and padding, and accessibility. It describes the current design; the reasons are in the decisions linked from each section. The IME's calls are in [ime-contract.md](ime-contract.md), and the place of `vtfx-compose` among the modules in section 3.5 of [architecture.md](architecture.md). How an app uses these features is in [usage.md](../usage.md).

## 1. Terms and routing of pointers

- **The display**: what one `Terminal` shows of its session, held by `TerminalDisplay` (one per `Terminal`): the scroll position in the scrollback, the selection, and when it redraws ([decisions/0084](../decisions/0084-session-and-threads.md)). Positions on the display are cells of the display, not of the screen.
- **The host asks for the mouse**: the mouse tracking mode is not off (modes 9, 1000, 1002, 1003).
- **Scrolled back**: the display shows lines of the scrollback, that is, its scroll offset is above 0 and the session has scrollback (`TerminalDisplay.isScrolledBack`). The alternate screen has no scrollback, so the offset that the main screen kept does not count there.
- **Reporting**: the host gets a pointer's input as mouse reports. It does when the host asks for the mouse and the display is not scrolled back, and, for a mouse, Shift is not held (`isReportingTouches` in `TerminalGestures.kt`, `isReporting` in `TerminalMouse.kt`). A report carries a row of the screen, so it cannot name a row of the scrollback ([decisions/0085](../decisions/0085-compose-interaction.md)).

A pointer whose `PointerType` is `Mouse` is handled by `terminalMouse` (section 3), over the grid and the inner padding (section 10). Its events are consumed in the initial pass, so the touch gestures and the ancestors' scrolling do not see them. Touch, stylus, and any other pointer are handled by `terminalGestures` on the grid (section 2) ([decisions/0085](../decisions/0085-compose-interaction.md)).

## 2. Touch

| Gesture | Not reporting, main screen | Not reporting, alternate screen | Reporting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tap | Drops the selection. On a link, calls `onLinkClick` (section 6); unless it takes the tap, hides the menu, focuses the terminal, and shows the software keyboard | Same as left | Drops the selection, reports a left press and release at the cell (links included), and shows the keyboard |
| Vertical swipe | Scrolls through the scrollback, a line per cell height moved | Sends Up or Down arrow keys, one per cell height moved (as xterm's alternateScroll) | Reports the wheel (buttons 64 and 65), one report per cell height moved, at the finger's cell |
| Long press | Selects the word under it; moving the finger extends the selection by words; lifting it shows the menu (section 4) at the finger and the selection handles (section 14) | Same as left | Same as left; drags are not reported |

- A long press is a press held for the long-press timeout without moving beyond the touch slop. Moving the finger extends the selection by words, as a mouse drag after a double click does (section 3): from the end of the pressed word farther from the finger to the far end of the word under the finger, a blank being a word of its own.
- Moving the finger down shows earlier content, the same direction as turning a wheel up.
- A swipe decides whether it reports when it starts: a swipe that scrolls the scrollback down to the bottom stops there instead of going on to scroll the host, and the next swipe goes to the host.
- The arrow keys of a swipe are sent with `TerminalSession.sendScrollKey`, without the sticky modifiers (section 8), which stay armed.
- If the display drops the selection during a long press, because its text changed (section 4), the rest of that long press selects nothing and shows no menu. Output that leaves the selected text as it is does not stop it: the finger extends the selection over what the display shows at each move, so if output scrolled the text, the selection still starts at the word the finger pressed.
- A stylus acts as touch; its taps report `PointerType.Stylus` to `onLinkClick`.
- While scrolled back, the display keeps showing the same lines as output arrives. When those lines leave the scrollback, it shows the oldest lines that are kept, or the screen if the scrollback was cleared. The user's input (a key, an IME edit, a paste, input sent with the session's API) returns the display to the bottom.

## 3. Mouse

| Input | Not reporting | Reporting |
|---|---|---|
| Wheel | Scrolls through the scrollback, 3 lines a notch; on the alternate screen, sends 3 arrow keys a notch (as a swipe) | Wheel buttons 64 and 65, one report a notch, at the pointer's cell |
| Two-finger swipe on a touchpad | As a vertical swipe on the grid (section 2): scrolls through the scrollback, a line per cell height moved; on the alternate screen, sends an arrow key per cell height moved | Wheel buttons 64 and 65, one report per cell height moved, at the pointer's cell |
| Primary button | A drag selects from the press to the pointer; a double click selects a word and a triple click a line (a logical line, through the rows that autowrap joins, within the display), and the drag that follows extends by words or lines; Shift+click extends the selection from its far end, by the unit that made it. A click without a drag drops the selection and is a tap: on a link it calls `onLinkClick`; unless it takes the click, the terminal is focused and the software keyboard requested | Press and release reported; the press drops the selection, and the terminal is focused, as by a tap |
| Secondary button | `onContextClick` if the app gave one, else the menu of the long press at the click. The selection stays | Press and release reported |
| Middle button | Nothing | Press and release reported |
| Motion | The pointer icon: a hand over a link (when `onLinkClick` is given), the I-beam elsewhere | With a button held: reported in modes 1002 and 1003 when the cell changes; without: reported in mode 1003 when the cell changes. The pointer icon is the arrow |

- **Shift** leaves the mouse to the terminal while the host asks for it, as in xterm: Shift+drag selects, Shift+wheel scrolls, and Shift+click on a link calls `onLinkClick`. Reports carry Alt and Ctrl (bits 8 and 16); Shift never reaches the host from the mouse.
- **Scrolled back**, the mouse is the terminal's even if the host asks for it, exactly as if Shift were held: a drag selects, the wheel scrolls, a click on a link calls `onLinkClick`, and the right click shows the menu or calls `onContextClick`. Once the display is back at the bottom, the host gets the mouse again: turning the wheel down to the bottom hands it back, and the next notch is reported.
  - A button's press decides for its drag and its release, so that the host never gets a press without its release or a release without its press: a press reported at the bottom is released to the host even if Shift+wheel scrolls back before the release, and a press made while scrolled back is not reported, nor is its release, even if the wheel reaches the bottom before the release. Each scroll event of the wheel decides for itself.
  - Motion without a button is not reported while scrolled back, and the pointer icon is the terminal's. After the wheel scrolls the scrollback, the icon follows without waiting for the pointer to move.
- **Wheel steps.** The wheel's delta (`scrollDelta`, 1 a notch, in fractions for high-resolution wheels and touchpads) is added up, and each whole notch scrolls. Horizontal scrolling (buttons 66 and 67) is not reported.
- **Encodings.** `vtfx-gate` encodes X10, normal, button-event, and any-event tracking in the default, UTF-8, SGR, and urxvt encodings, and drops the events that the mode does not ask for. Motion is sent only when the cell changes, so that a fine mouse does not flood the host.
- **Multiple clicks** count when a press comes within the double-tap timeout of the last one, on the same cell, and go round from a triple click to a single one.
- **Dragging by words or lines.** As after a long press (section 2), after a double or triple click, the button held down extends the selection by the unit of the press: from the end of the word or line that the press selected farther from the pointer, to the far end of the word or line under the pointer (a blank is a word of its own, section 4). Back within the pressed word or line, the selection is that word or line ([decisions/0085](../decisions/0085-compose-interaction.md)).
- **Shift+click by the unit of the selection.** Shift+click, and the drag that follows it, extend the selection by the unit of the press that made it: by words after a double click or a long press, by lines after a triple click, and by cells after a drag or Select all. The far end stays where it is, and the selection reaches the far end of the word or line at the click.
- **No menu after a mouse selection**; the right click shows it.
- **Focus and the software keyboard.** A click focuses the terminal and asks for the software keyboard, as a tap does. With a hardware keyboard attached, Android normally does not show it, so vtfx does not tell desktop-like devices apart.
- **`onContextClick: ((Offset) -> Unit)?`** gets the position in pixels from the top left of the `Terminal` (within the padding of its `modifier`). The app reads `TerminalState.hasSelection` (section 5) to offer Copy.
- **If the display drops the selection during a drag**, the rest of the drag selects nothing, as with a long press. Before the first move there is no selection to drop, unless a double or triple click made one, and the drag stops only if the lines are numbered again (section 4). Shift+click extends the selection also from an end that scrolled off the display.
- **Touchpads.** Android reports a touchpad's taps and pointer moves with `PointerType.Touch` or `Mouse` depending on the device. From Android 14, a two-finger swipe comes as a fake finger classified as a two-finger swipe, which Compose gives as a pan of the mouse (`PointerEventType.PanStart`, `PanMove`, and `PanEnd`, with the distance in `panOffset`). Before Android 14, or on other devices, it may come as scroll events, which act as the wheel, or as a touch swipe (section 2). A pan acts as a swipe on the grid, not as the wheel: it moves in pixels, as a finger does, and it decides when it starts whether the host gets it, so that a two-finger swipe down to the bottom of the scrollback stops there. Shift held at its start leaves it to the terminal, as for the wheel. Whether a touchpad behaves as a mouse is not verified (section 12).

## 4. Selection and copying

- **Where it is held.** The selection is held by the `TerminalDisplay`: an anchor and a focus cell, inclusive, in either order (`TerminalSelection`), each a column of a line numbered as the search numbers lines (section 13), so that a cell keeps its number as its line scrolls into the scrollback. A session shown by two `Terminal`s has two selections, and a session without a UI has none ([decisions/0085](../decisions/0085-compose-interaction.md)).
- **It stays on its text.** Output scrolls the selection with its text, into the scrollback and out of the display, and scrolling the display does not change it. The display highlights the part that it shows, and Copy copies all of it.
- **When it is dropped** ([decisions/0085](../decisions/0085-compose-interaction.md)):
  - When its text changes: at each redraw after output, the display compares the selected cells of the lines that the screen held at the last check with what those lines hold now, whether they are still on the screen or scrolled into the scrollback since. It compares the characters, their widths, and whether they are concealed, as copying reads them, and whether autowrap joins a selected line to the next selected one; a change of style alone, or a change outside the selected columns, keeps the selection. Lines of the scrollback do not change, so they are not read again, and a check reads at most about a screen of lines.
  - When the lines are numbered again: on resizing, which reflows them, on clearing the scrollback, and on switching between the main and the alternate screen. The alternate screen has no scrollback, so when a program scrolls it (vim, less), the text under the selection changes and the selection is dropped.
  - When all its lines leave the scrollback, because of its limit. When only its first lines leave, the selection is cut to start at the oldest line kept.
  - On the user's input (a key, an IME edit, a paste), which returns the display to the bottom; on a tap and a mouse click without a drag; and on the menu's Copy (below).
- **Units.** A selection keeps the unit of the press that made it, which a drag or a Shift+click extends it by (sections 2 and 3). A word is the run of non-blank characters around a cell, or the cell alone if it is blank. A line is a logical line: its rows from the one that the previous row does not wrap into to the one that does not wrap, within the display. There is no block selection and no keyboard mode for selecting ([decisions/0079](../decisions/0079-emulation-scope.md)).
- **The copied text.** The trailing blanks of each line are dropped, and lines are separated by `\n`, except rows that autowrap joined, which are joined without one. A wide character is included whole even if only half of it is selected. The blank that autowrap leaves before a wide character that did not fit is not text. Concealed cells (SGR 8), which the display does not draw, are copied as blanks, so that copying what the user sees does not give text the user did not see ([decisions/0083](../decisions/0083-gate.md)).
- **The copy path.** Copying goes through `TerminalSession.copyToClipboard` (internal API) to `TerminalGate.copyToClipboard` in `vtfx-gate`, which writes to the `ClipboardAccess` given to the session; it returns false when there is no clipboard. It is the user's action, so the OSC 52 policy does not apply ([decisions/0083](../decisions/0083-gate.md)).
- **The menu.** A long press, or a right click without `onContextClick`, shows the text toolbar with Copy (only while there is a selection; it drops the selection, as the menu goes away), Paste (`TerminalSession.pasteFromClipboard`), and Select all, which selects all and shows the menu again to copy, with the selection handles if a long press opened the menu (section 14).
- **Select all** selects from the oldest line of the scrollback to the last row of the screen; on the alternate screen, which has no scrollback, only the screen. The display highlights all that it shows. Like any selection, it stays on its lines, so lines that output adds below are not selected; since it covers the whole screen, output that changes any text on the screen drops it. Empty lines at its end are left out of the copied text.
- **Reading the copied text.** The text of a selection is read from its lines at once under the session's lock (`TerminalSession.searchLines`, internal): at most its newest 10,000 lines and, to bound the memory on the main thread, at most 2 Mi cells (`MAX_COPIED_LINES`, `MAX_COPIED_CELLS` in `TerminalDisplay`). The text is cut as below.
- **The length of a copy.** Any copied text is cut to the newest whole lines that fit in 256 Ki characters (`MAX_COPIED_LENGTH` in `TerminalDisplay`), or to the end of a single line longer than that, without splitting a surrogate pair: a clip goes to the system in one Binder transaction, which fails above about 1 MB, and 256 Ki characters are 512 KB of UTF-16. A selection of a single screen can reach that too, since a cell holds up to 32 UTF-16 units that the host chooses.
- **Synchronized output.** During synchronized output (mode 2026), the display defers redraws, and so the check of the selection, for up to 200 ms, while copying and the link of a click read the current lines. The host can therefore change the text under a selection, or the OSC 8 link of a cell, without the user seeing it for that window. This is accepted ([decisions/0085](../decisions/0085-compose-interaction.md)).

## 5. `TerminalState`

`TerminalState` is the app's handle on the selection, for its own Copy command or shortcut ([decisions/0085](../decisions/0085-compose-interaction.md)), and on the search (section 13).

- Created with `rememberTerminalState()` and passed as `Terminal(session, state = state)`. By default, `Terminal` remembers one that the app cannot reach, with `rememberTerminalState()` too.
- `hasSelection` is backed by snapshot state, so a composable that reads it (an enabled Copy item) is recomposed when the selection changes.
- `copySelection()` copies as the menu's Copy does (section 4), but keeps the selection, as Ctrl+C in a text field and Ctrl+Shift+C in desktop terminals do; it returns whether there was a selection to copy and a clipboard to copy it to. `selectAll()` selects as the menu's Select all does. `clearSelection()` drops the selection.
- Not attached to a `Terminal` (before it enters the composition, after it leaves), `hasSelection` is false and the functions do nothing. Passed to two terminals at once, it acts on the one that entered the composition last; when that one leaves, it acts on none until a terminal enters again.
- Main thread only, as Compose state is, without a run-time check.
- **Leaving the composition.** When the terminal it acts on leaves the composition, as when the app switches tabs or screens, the state keeps what the display showed (`SavedView`): the number of the line at the top of the display, or that it showed the bottom, the generation of the line numbers, the query, and the current match. The next display that the state is attached to takes it: if its session is the same and the generation has not changed, it shows the same line at its top, or the oldest line kept if that line left the scrollback, and the same current match if it still matches; otherwise it shows the bottom. Either way it searches for the same query (section 13). Lines are numbered so that a line keeps its number as output pushes it into the scrollback, so output while the terminal is away does not move what it shows when it comes back. The selection is not kept: whether its text changed while away cannot be checked without the lines it held, which the state does not keep ([decisions/0095](../decisions/0095-keeping-the-view.md)).
- **The instance state.** `rememberTerminalState()` remembers the state with `rememberSaveable`, saving the same values, so that the view survives a configuration change and a `SaveableStateHolder` (Navigation's back stack, tabs that keep their screens' state). The saved view names its session by a random key that the display gives each session it shows, held weakly on the main thread, so that a view saved before the process was killed does not match the new session that the app creates, even with the same lines and numbers; such a view gives only its query.

## 6. Links

### 6.1 Kinds of links and clicks

- `TerminalLink` is an abstract class with an internal constructor, so that only vtfx makes links and a `when` over the kinds needs an `else` branch ([decisions/0087](../decisions/0087-public-api.md)). Its kinds are `Explicit(text, hyperlink)`, an OSC 8 hyperlink, whose `text` is that of the cells around the clicked one with the same hyperlink, joined across the rows that autowrap joins within the display (the blank that autowrap leaves before a wide character that did not fit is skipped), with concealed cells as blanks; and `Matched(text, url, pattern)`, text that a pattern matched (section 6.2). Built-in URLs are `Matched` with `pattern == LinkPattern.Urls`, and their `url` is their `text`.
- `onLinkClick: ((TerminalLinkClick) -> Boolean)?` gets a `TerminalLinkClick`: the `link`, the `pointerType` of the pointer that clicked, and the `keyboardModifiers` held, Compose's own types, so that an app can open links only on Ctrl+click with a mouse and still on a tap ([decisions/0085](../decisions/0085-compose-interaction.md)). It returns whether it took the click. A click that it takes only hides the menu; one that it does not take is a click off links: it hides the menu, focuses the terminal, and shows the software keyboard. Whether links are underlined, matched, and get the hand pointer depends only on whether `onLinkClick` is given, not on what it returns, so an app that takes only Ctrl+click keeps the links drawn the same while Ctrl is up. A tap reports `PointerType.Touch` and the modifiers of its release event. Activation without a pointer would report `PointerType.Unknown` and `PointerKeyboardModifiers(0)`; no such path exists, since a screen reader's double tap on a row becomes a tap (section 11).
- `TerminalLink`'s kinds and `TerminalLinkClick` are ordinary classes with internal constructors and value equality, not data classes, so that properties can be added ([decisions/0087](../decisions/0087-public-api.md)).
- A click reaches `onLinkClick` only when it is not reporting (sections 2 and 3). With a mouse while the host asks for the mouse, a click goes to the host, links included, unless Shift is held; then it calls `onLinkClick` with Shift in the modifiers. Ctrl+click goes to the host then (with the Ctrl bit), so an app that opens links on Ctrl+click opens them only while the host does not ask for the mouse, or on Ctrl+Shift+click. While scrolled back, every click is the terminal's, and calls `onLinkClick` with the modifiers held, Ctrl included.
- vtfx never opens a link from a click. The app shows the URL, and calls `TerminalSession.openUrl` when the user confirms; the gate checks the URL (section 5.2 of [gate-policy.md](gate-policy.md)). This holds for every kind of link: an OSC 8 hyperlink's text and URL come from the host apart, and text in the background's color or out of view can be part of a match ([decisions/0087](../decisions/0087-public-api.md)).
- Without `onLinkClick`, links are not clickable, and the patterns are not matched, so only OSC 8 hyperlinks, which are always underlined, look like links.

### 6.2 Link patterns

- `LinkPattern(regex, urlTemplate)` (`vtfx-core`): `{0}` in the template is the whole match and `{n}` group n (an empty string if the group did not match). Other braces, and groups that the regular expression does not have, are rejected at construction.
- Each value put into the template is percent-encoded, so that the matched text, which the host decides, cannot change the host of the URL or add segments to it. A value of `.` or `..` stays as it is, and makes a dot segment that opening the URL removes, with the segment before it for `..` ([decisions/0087](../decisions/0087-public-api.md)).
- `LinkPattern.Urls` finds the `http` and `https` URLs in the text with a linear scan without backtracking. The URL is the text itself, without the punctuation that usually ends a sentence and closing brackets without an opening one; only ASCII characters allowed in URLs are taken.
- Two patterns are equal if they have the same regular expression, options, and template, so a fresh list with equal patterns passed on each recomposition does not redo detection.
- `Terminal(linkPatterns = ...)` takes the patterns in order of priority; the default is `listOf(LinkPattern.Urls)`, and an empty list stops detection.

### 6.3 Finding links

`LinkFinder` (`vtfx-core`, internal API) finds the links on the display.

- Rows that autowrap joins are matched as one text, without the blank that autowrap leaves before a wide character that did not fit.
- Where matches overlap, the earlier pattern wins. Overlaps are decided on the text alone; then a match that covers a cell with an OSC 8 hyperlink, or a concealed cell (SGR 8), is not a link, and the matches of later patterns that it overlapped stay dropped. So a later pattern never makes a link inside an earlier pattern's match, and the OSC 8 hyperlink of a cell always wins. A concealed cell is not drawn, so a match over it would go to a URL that the user did not see ([decisions/0087](../decisions/0087-public-api.md)).
- A logical line that the display cuts at its top or bottom is matched with its rows beyond the display, which the snapshot keeps, up to one screen of rows each way; only the part inside the display is a link. Where the line goes on beyond those rows too, a match that reaches the cut is not a link, since the text beyond could change it.
- Links are found on the main thread at every draw. The links of each logical line are kept by the line's content from one draw to the next, so only lines whose content changed are matched again. Overlaps are removed in time linear in the number of matches.
- vtfx sets no bound on the work of the app's regular expressions, which run on text from the host, and Android's regular expressions cannot be stopped once started. Giving patterns that take time linear in the text is the app's responsibility, as the KDoc of `LinkPattern` and [usage.md](../usage.md) say ([decisions/0087](../decisions/0087-public-api.md)).
- The underlines of matched links are drawn over the line, outside the per-line drawing cache, like the selection: a match depends on the rows that autowrap joins, so it cannot be part of a line's cache key. OSC 8 hyperlinks are underlined with the line's cells. Their styles are in [decisions/0079](../decisions/0079-emulation-scope.md).
- Hovering a mouse over a link changes only the pointer icon, to a hand (section 3).

## 7. Hardware keys

### 7.1 Keys to the host

Hardware keys go to the focused terminal. `TerminalInputView` passes the keys that the IME did not consume to `KeyEventHandler`, which handles them by the same rules as the keys that the IME sends with `sendKeyEvent` (section 6.3 of [ime-contract.md](ime-contract.md)).

- Escape, Tab, Enter, the keys of the numeric keypad, Backspace (DEL), Insert, Forward Delete, Home, End, Page Up, Page Down, the arrows, and F1 to F12 are terminal keys, with Shift, Alt, Ctrl, and Meta as modifiers.
- Any other key is the character that it produces with Shift applied, with Shift, Alt, Ctrl, and Meta as modifiers. The encoding without the kitty keyboard protocol ignores Shift for characters; the protocol reports it (section 2.2 of [gate-policy.md](gate-policy.md)). The key also carries, for the kitty keyboard protocol, its unshifted key, the character that it produces without modifiers in the keyboard's key character map (or, if it produces none, the character in lower case), and the key of the base layout, the key at the same place on a US PC-101 keyboard, from the Linux scan code ([decisions/0083](../decisions/0083-gate.md)). A key that produces no character, or a dead key (a combining accent), is not a terminal key and is not consumed (Back, for example).
- A release is consumed for the keys whose presses are, and has no effect.
- With NumLock off, the digits and the decimal point of the keypad are its navigation keys (Keypad Insert for 0 to Keypad Page Up for 9, and Keypad Delete), which the kitty keyboard protocol tells apart ([decisions/0083](../decisions/0083-gate.md)) and the encoding without it sends as the digits at their places, ignoring NumLock.
- The numeric keypad sends its characters, Keypad Enter following LNM; with the application keypad (DECKPAM, or DECNKM, mode 66), it sends SS3 sequences, as xterm does with NumLock off, and `CSI 1 ; m` sequences with modifiers ([decisions/0085](../decisions/0085-compose-interaction.md)). While the host set flag 1 of the kitty keyboard protocol, it sends the forms of section 2.2 of [gate-policy.md](gate-policy.md) instead.

### 7.2 Keys for the app

`Terminal(onPreImeKeyEvent = ...)` takes `((KeyEvent) -> Boolean)?` (Compose's `KeyEvent`), null by default ([decisions/0085](../decisions/0085-compose-interaction.md)).

- `TerminalInputView.dispatchKeyEventPreIme` calls it with each key event that the window dispatches to the focused view before the IME: presses, repeated presses, and releases of hardware keys, only while the terminal has the focus. With several terminals, only the focused terminal's callback is called.
- Returning true consumes the event: the window then sends it neither to the IME nor to `dispatchKeyEvent`, so it does not reach the session. The KDoc asks the app to consume a key's release with its press, and to return false for the keys it does not use, including system keys such as Back, which the IME uses to hide itself.
- Keys from a software keyboard and keys sent with `TerminalSession.sendKey` do not pass through it.
- `Modifier.onPreviewKeyEvent` on an ancestor of `Terminal` sees only the keys that the IME passes on: Android dispatches a hardware key to the views' `dispatchKeyEventPreIme`, then to the IME, and only then, for the keys the IME did not consume, to the views' `dispatchKeyEvent`, where Compose runs `onPreviewKeyEvent`. Gboard consumes the keys of a physical keyboard and resends them through the `InputConnection`. The KDoc of `Terminal` and [usage.md](../usage.md) therefore recommend `onPreImeKeyEvent`.
- Robolectric has no IME, so `TerminalKeyInterceptionTest` dispatches as the window does: before the IME, then, unless consumed, after it.

## 8. Extra key bar and sticky modifiers

- The library has no key bar UI (requirement 5.4). The app builds one with `TerminalSession.sendKey`, `sendText`, `paste`, and `stickyModifiers` (section 5 of [usage.md](../usage.md)); the sample app has a key bar with Esc, Tab, Ctrl, Alt, the arrows, Direct, and Find ([decisions/0085](../decisions/0085-compose-interaction.md)).
- `stickyModifiers` apply to the next key, or to the first character of the next text that the IME editing model sends, and are then cleared. An HT, as that first character or as a character key, takes them as the Tab key (Shift+Tab is CSI Z), and a character key of a line break as the Enter key: the key encoding drops a control character with modifiers. `TerminalSessionListener.onStickyModifiersChanged` reports each change, including the clearing. When the modifiers change a key or a character, the host no longer gets what the IME's input history predicts, so the input history is reset after the edit (section 5.1 of [ime-contract.md](ime-contract.md)).
- Arming sticky modifiers (setting modifiers that are not empty and differ from the current ones) first commits the composing text and sends it, with the edits not sent yet, without the new modifiers, as a key would before itself. The input history is then reset, so that the IME, which may still have the composing text, restarts, and can no longer correct the text typed before arming, which would be sent again with the modifiers. The modifiers then apply only to what is typed after arming, composing text included. Clearing them sends nothing ([decisions/0082](../decisions/0082-ime-editing-model.md)).
  - The pending input is sent even during a batch edit of the IME, as a paste is, since the app's call is not part of the IME's edit; the input history is emptied at once, and only the restart of the IME waits for the end of the batch (section 5.2 of [ime-contract.md](ime-contract.md)).
  - The reset restarts the IME only if it saw an edit since the last restart, so that tapping Ctrl with nothing typed does not restart the IME every time (section 5.2 of [ime-contract.md](ime-contract.md)).
  - If modifiers are already armed and the app adds another one, the composing text typed after the first takes the first, as it would have when it was committed.
- A deletion that is part of an IME edit (`deleteSurroundingText`) is sent as plain DELs and does not take the sticky modifiers; BS as a key event (`KEYCODE_DEL`) does ([decisions/0082](../decisions/0082-ime-editing-model.md)).
- The keys of scroll gestures (section 2) do not take the sticky modifiers, which stay armed.

## 9. Cursor blinking

- The cursor blinks only when the cursor style blinks, and only while the terminal is focused: it is shown and hidden every 0.5 seconds. The style is the host's (DECSCUSR, mode 12), or, until the host sets it, the app's default (`TerminalStyle.cursorBlinking`, off by default; [decisions/0079](../decisions/0079-emulation-scope.md)). The character blink attribute (SGR 5) is not drawn ([decisions/0085](../decisions/0085-compose-interaction.md)).
- Each input from the user (a key, an IME edit, a paste, or input sent with the session's API) shows the cursor and starts its phase again, so that the cursor stays on while the user types, also when the host does not echo. So does output from the host that moves the cursor, as in xterm.js; output that leaves the cursor where it was, such as a redraw that returns it, does not. The position is compared each time the display updates.

## 10. Size and inner padding of `Terminal`

### 10.1 Default size

`Terminal` prefers 80 × 24 cells (xterm's default, and `TerminalSession`'s), the way `Text` prefers the size of its text: the size is clamped to the incoming constraints, so fixed constraints (`size`, `fillMaxSize`, `weight`) decide it, loose ones give 80 × 24 cells or their maximum, and unbounded ones (inside a scrolling container) give 80 × 24 cells (`preferredGridSize` in `Terminal.kt`) ([decisions/0085](../decisions/0085-compose-interaction.md)).

- A grid with no width or height: `Terminal` logs a warning with `android.util.Log` (tag `vtfx`) and does not resize the session, which keeps its size. A size that is not zero but holds less than one cell gives one column or row.
- How the session follows the size, after a delay, is in section 3.5 of [architecture.md](architecture.md) ([decisions/0084](../decisions/0084-session-and-threads.md)).

### 10.2 Inner padding

`TerminalStyle.padding: PaddingValues`, zero by default ([decisions/0085](../decisions/0085-compose-interaction.md)).

- The padding is drawn in the grid's default background color, the host's if it set one (OSC 11, section 12 of [emulation.md](emulation.md)), swapped with the foreground in reverse video (DECSCNM), as the renderer swaps the grid's.
- The grid's `Canvas`, the `TerminalInputView` for the IME, and the rows for accessibility are all inset by `Modifier.padding`. Their coordinates stay the grid's, so the touch gestures, the long-press menu's anchor, and the `CursorAnchorInfo` need no offset. The columns and rows are counted from the inset size, and the default size adds the padding.
- `Modifier.padding` follows the layout direction, so `start` is on the right in right-to-left layouts. The default size and the tap detector of the padding round the padding to pixels as `Modifier.padding` does.
- A tap in the padding focuses the terminal and shows the keyboard, as a tap on the grid does; long presses and drags in the padding do nothing. The mouse acts over the padding as over the grid (section 3), at the nearest cell.
- Each change of the style makes the paints and the cell size again, padding included.

## 11. Accessibility

[decisions/0085](../decisions/0085-compose-interaction.md) explains this section.

- **A node per row.** Each displayed row is one semantics node with the position and size of the row, and its `text` is the row's text without trailing blanks, with concealed cells (SGR 8) as blanks, as in copying ([decisions/0083](../decisions/0083-gate.md)). Screen readers move row by row with swipes, and read the touched row with explore by touch. When the display is scrolled back, the rows of the scrollback that it shows are exposed. The rows are laid out by `RowSemantics` in `Terminal.kt`, over the grid without handling touches, and each row's semantics are those of a `TerminalRowSemanticsNode`.
- **Computed only when queried.** The rows' text is made only when an accessibility service (or a test) queries the semantics, once per change of the display, and shared by the row nodes. Without a service, drawing costs nothing more.
- **No click action on rows.** Compose would report the bounds of a clickable node enlarged to the minimum touch target, and the rows, shorter than that, would overlap. A screen reader's double tap on a row without a click action is sent by explore by touch as a tap at the focus position, so it focuses the terminal and shows the keyboard as a tap does.
- **The view for the IME is not exposed.** `TerminalInputView` is `IMPORTANT_FOR_ACCESSIBILITY_NO`. The IME connects through input focus, apart from the accessibility tree, so the IME's editing buffer and the text that the screen reader reads do not constrain each other (requirement 6.3).
- **Rows are sensitive data.** The row nodes have Compose's `isSensitiveData`. On Android 14 (API 34) and later, they are shown only to services that declare `isAccessibilityTool` (TalkBack, Switch Access, and so on): Compose sets `AccessibilityNodeInfo.setAccessibilityDataSensitive`, and returns no node for the requests of other services. On API 29 to 33, the platform cannot tell a tool from another service, and every accessibility service sees the rows ([decisions/0090](../decisions/0090-distribution.md)).
- **Content Capture and screen sharing are the app's choice.** Compose's Content Capture does not look at `isSensitiveData`, and the means of stopping it affects the whole process; `Modifier.sensitiveContent` would also hide the terminal when it should be shared. vtfx sets neither, and section 6 of [usage.md](../usage.md) tells apps how.
- **No live regions.** New output is not read automatically.

## 13. Search

[decisions/0085](../decisions/0085-compose-interaction.md) explains this section. The app has the search field; the terminal finds the matches, highlights them, and scrolls to them.

- **Where it is held.** The search is held by the `TerminalDisplay`, as the selection is (section 4): a session shown by two `Terminal`s can be searched in each apart, and the display's search, with the text it keeps, goes when its `Terminal` leaves the composition; the `TerminalState` keeps the query and the current match and gives them to the next `Terminal` (section 5). `TerminalState` is the app's handle on it (section 5): `search(text, ignoreCase)`, `findNext()`, `findPrevious()`, `clearSearch()`, and `searchResult`, a snapshot state with the number of matches, whether the number is capped, and the index of the current match from the oldest. Not attached to a `Terminal`, `searchResult` is null and the functions do nothing.
- **What is searched.** The scrollback and the screen, or only the screen on the alternate screen. Rows that autowrap joins are one text, read as links are (section 6.3): a match does not cross a line break, and the blank that autowrap leaves before a wide character is not text. A line that autowrap joins through more than 100 rows is searched in parts of 100 rows, counted from where the search started reading it, and a match does not cross from one part to the next (`MAX_SEARCH_TEXT_ROWS`, [decisions/0081](../decisions/0081-resource-limits.md)). The blanks at the end of a line are not text, as in copying (section 4). A match covers the cells of its characters, a wide character whole.
- **Matching.** The text as it is, ignoring the case of letters if asked, compared one UTF-16 unit at a time as `String.regionMatches` compares them. Matches do not overlap: each is the first that starts after the previous one ends. An occurrence that covers a concealed cell (SGR 8) is not a match, and an occurrence that overlaps it may still be. `SearchIndex` in `vtfx-core` (internal API) finds them with Knuth-Morris-Pratt, in time linear in the text and the query. Empty text ends the search.
- **At most 1,000 matches**, the newest (`SearchIndex.MAX_MATCHES`). `TerminalSearchResult.isMatchCountCapped` says when there are more, and the index counts from the oldest match kept.
- **The current match.** A search makes current the nearest match at or above the current one, or above the bottom of the display when there is none, or else the first match below, so that typing more of the text keeps the match while it still matches. `findPrevious` moves up and `findNext` down, each going round from one end to the other. Each of them scrolls the display to show the current match, near its middle, unless the display already shows it whole; the selection stays on its text, as it does when the user scrolls.
- **Following the terminal.** At each redraw after output (section 4), the search reads the lines that entered the scrollback since and the screen, with the part of a line that goes on into the screen (fewer than 100 rows of the scrollback), a thousand lines at a time with the session's lock held, and keeps the matches of the older lines: lines in the scrollback do not change. Output may scroll between two reads; so that a redraw ends when the output scrolls faster than the reads go, a redraw reads at most about twice as many times as reading the lines kept at its first read takes, and leaves the rest to the next redraw ([decisions/0081](../decisions/0081-resource-limits.md)). Matches go with the lines that leave the scrollback. If the current match goes, the nearest one above it becomes current. Output and the user's input do not scroll the display for the search, and do not end it. Resizing (which reflows the lines), clearing the scrollback (ED 3), and switching between the main and the alternate screen number the lines again, and the search reads them all again, keeping its query.
- **Lines are numbered** by the count of lines pushed into the scrollback, as image placements in the scrollback are: the first row of the screen is that count, and a line keeps its number in the scrollback. `TerminalSnapshot.firstLine` (internal) numbers the display's first row the same way, so that the matches are drawn on the rows that show them.
- **What is kept.** While searching, the text of the lines of the scrollback, without the blanks at the end of each line, with two bytes for the column of each character, so that a new query searches it without reading the lines again. It goes with the search.
- **Drawing.** The matches that the display shows are filled with `TerminalColors.searchMatch`, and the current one with `searchCurrentMatch`, translucent, over the text and the link underlines and under the selection, outside the per-line drawing cache like the selection.

## 14. Selection handles

[decisions/0097](../decisions/0097-selection-handles.md) explains this section. A selection made by touch shows a teardrop handle at each end, which the user drags to move that end, as in Android's text fields.

- **When.** The handles show when the finger of a long press is lifted, after Select all of a menu that a long press opened, and while a handle is dragged. They go when the selection is set in any other way, or dropped: a tap, a mouse selection or click, the user's input, `TerminalState.selectAll()` and `clearSelection()`, and the display's checks (section 4). A selection that the display cuts to the lines left in the scrollback keeps them (`TerminalDisplay.showsHandles`).
- **Look.** Each handle is a 25 dp circle with the square corner of its top quadrant toward the text, as Compose's text fields draw theirs, in `LocalTextSelectionColors.current.handleColor`, which a Material theme sets for the app's text fields. Its tip is the bottom left corner of the first selected cell for the start handle, and the bottom right corner of the last for the end handle. The start handle hangs left of its tip and points at the top right, and the end handle right of its tip and points at the top left; a handle that would leave the window at its side hangs on the other side of its tip, pointing the other way, as `TextView` turns its handles: so the start handle of a selection from the first column, in a terminal at the window's left edge, is drawn as the end handle is.
- **Where.** Each handle is in its own `Popup` that does not take the focus, so that it may hang below the grid and beyond the terminal, which clips what it draws, and so that the terminal keeps the IME's input connection. A handle is not shown while its end is off the display, or while its tip is outside the part of the grid that the ancestors leave visible (`SelectionHandles.kt`).
- **Dragging.** A handle moves its end by cells, to the cell whose edge is nearest to the tip, on the row above the tip; the tip keeps the distance from the finger that the press had. The finger's position is taken through the screen's coordinates, as the popup moves with the handle. The end does not pass the other end, so at least one cell stays selected, and a wide character is taken whole at the moved end (`TerminalSelection.withEndAt`). The selection then extends by cells (section 3). Each move to another cell gives the haptic feedback `TextHandleMove`, as Compose's handles do. Beyond the top or bottom of the display, the end stops at the first or last row, and the display does not scroll: a swipe scrolls it, and the selection stays.
- **The menu.** A drag hides the menu, and lifting the handle shows it again at the row of the dragged end. While the handles show, the rectangle given to the text toolbar covers the row and the handles below it, so that a menu placed below the row, when there is no room above, does not cover the handles, as in `TextView`.
- **Reporting.** The handles act the same when the host asks for the mouse, as the long press does (section 2); their drags are not reported.
